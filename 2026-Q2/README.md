# 2026 Q1 performance review & 2026 Q2 allocation

As a reminder, the Contrarian Momentum Portfolio is a multi-asset strategy driven by macroeconomic views, including geographical, sectoral, thematic, currency, interest rate, and credit considerations. The portfolio maintains an average allocation of 60% to equities, ensuring that variations in equity exposure over time do not distort comparisons with the benchmark.

Flexibility in asset allocation primarily comes from the remaining 40% invested across other asset classes—namely fixed income, credit, and precious metals—which are actively managed to generate excess returns relative to the benchmark based on discretionary macro views.

## 2026 Q1 performance review

In the first quarter, the momentum-contrarian portfolio delivered a return of +1.1%, compared with -1.1% for the benchmark*. While the portfolio exhibited higher volatility and a greater maximum drawdown, these metrics should be assessed over the full period since the strategy’s inception in Q1 2025. (The strategy has been live since early 2025, with allocations published on this GitHub only from Q1 2026 onward). 

Performance was primarily weighed down by emerging market exposures, notably in Indonesia, India, and Vietnam. The Indian rupee depreciated by approximately 2% against the US dollar, despite the central bank’s substantial foreign exchange reserves, which had been expected to support the currency. In Indonesia, inflation came in above expectations at the start of the year, and the investment case currently lacks near-term catalysts despite the country’s strong long-term fundamentals.

Monthly attribution indicates that most assets detracted from performance in March, amid market reactions to the prolonged blockade of the Strait of Hormuz. The portfolio maintained a deliberate cyclical bias through long positions in emerging markets (both equities and currencies versus the US dollar), which negatively impacted returns during the month. More unexpectedly, gold behaved like a risk asset rather than fulfilling its traditional role as a safe haven. 

Nevertheless, precious metals contributed positively over the quarter, alongside several other segments of the portfolio, including utilities, mining, and, to a lesser extent, semiconductors.

*Benchmark is 65% equity / 35% bonds (ACWI Net Return €: NDEEWNR Index & Global Aggregate Net Return € Hedged: LEGATREH Index)

## 2026 Q2 allocation

For the second quarter and the remainder of 2026, I believe a more cautious stance is warranted. The blockade of the Strait of Hormuz appears likely to persist, given that the Revolutionary Guards have been preparing for a protracted conflict for decades. In this context, I do not view a near-term de-escalation as the base case, despite what current market pricing seems to imply. Indeed, both Brent and WTI futures are pricing lower oil prices at the end of 2026 than a week ago, suggesting a degree of complacency.

Looking beyond 2026, I anticipate a return to a more pronounced stagflationary environment in developed economies. In my view, central banks are unlikely to respond with aggressive rate hikes, as this represents a supply-side shock which, if sustained, would ultimately erode demand and weigh on growth.

Against this backdrop, I am reducing exposure to the most cyclical segments of the portfolio, notably European banks and semiconductors, previously held via ETFs. I am also scaling back short USD positions against major and emerging market currencies (including EUR, JPY, CHF, and EM FX). At the same time, I am increasing allocations to fixed income to retain some sensitivity to long-term rates in the event of a sharp growth slowdown. However, the most significant shift is an increase in cash, reflecting limited conviction on the direction of yield curves in a stagflationary regime.

I am also increasing exposure to Chinese A-shares. I believe China could emerge relatively stronger from the current geopolitical environment, as the United States’ perceived instability may encourage a number of countries—particularly emerging economies—to deepen their ties with alternative blocs, notably China. In addition, recent producer price data suggest a gradual return of inflationary pressures, which could support revenues for domestically oriented companies. Despite still-subdued consumer confidence, Chinese retail investors are allocating increasing capital to domestic equities. Even a modest improvement in sentiment could result in significant inflows.

I am also increasing my exposure to the United States, where EPS revisions continue to trend upward, and which appears more resilient to a potential escalation of the conflict, at least from a fundamental perspective.

On a sectoral basis, I am further increasing exposure to utilities, particularly in the United States. I do not believe this theme is exhausted. I expect global technology firms to continue acquiring energy assets in both the US and Europe, in order to address what is emerging as a key bottleneck in the deployment of productivity gains associated with generative and agent-based AI.
